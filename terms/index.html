Safety — the most important section

Surfing carries real risk. Drowning, head injuries from boards or rocks, marine animals, hypothermia, and fatigue are not theoretical. Beginners are at higher risk than experienced surfers because they read conditions less accurately, fall more often, and tire faster.

WaxUp is an information tool, not a coach, lifeguard, or emergency service. We translate forecast data and provide beginner-friendly context. Every decision to enter the water is yours. Conditions change faster than any forecast can predict. A “good” rating from WaxUp does not guarantee safe surf — and a “poor” rating does not mean disaster, just that the day is unlikely to be good for you.

You agree to:

  • Never surf alone if you are a beginner.
  • Surf at lifeguarded beaches when you can.
  • Wear a leash. Wear a wetsuit appropriate to the water temperature.
  • Know the local hazards (rocks, rip currents, marine life, boat traffic).
  • Check conditions in person before paddling out, even after consulting WaxUp.
  • Stay within your skill level. Walk away if conditions exceed it.
  • Not surf under the influence of alcohol or drugs.
  • Follow lifeguard instructions and local water-safety rules.

Water quality. WaxUp surfaces water quality warnings derived from public data and recent rainfall. These warnings are advisory. We do not test water ourselves. Always defer to official health and beach closure notices from local authorities.

Rip currents. WaxUp displays National Weather Service rip current forecasts where available. The NWS data is provided as-is. We do not modify their predictions and we cannot guarantee accuracy.

WaxUp does not call emergency services for you. If you or someone else is in trouble in the water, call your local emergency number (911 / 999 / 000 / 112).
